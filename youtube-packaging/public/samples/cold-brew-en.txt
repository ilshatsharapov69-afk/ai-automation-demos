Hey everyone, welcome back to the channel. Today we're making cold brew at home, and I'll show you the three mistakes that made my first batches taste bitter and flat.
0:42 Picking the beans
Cold brew hides a lot of flaws, but not stale coffee. Medium or dark roast beans roasted in the last month give the sweetest cup. You don't need expensive single origin beans for cold brew at home.
2:05 Grind size
This is mistake number one. A fine grind makes cold brew muddy and bitter. Go coarse, like raw sugar. If your grinder has numbers, start near the coarsest setting and adjust from there.
3:40 The ratio
Mistake number two is guessing the ratio. Use one part coffee to eight parts water for a ready-to-drink cold brew, or one to four for a concentrate you dilute later. Weigh it once and you'll never guess again.
5:15 Steeping time
Steep it in the fridge for 12 to 18 hours. Less than 12 tastes weak, more than 20 starts to taste woody. Mistake number three is leaving it on the counter all weekend.
7:02 Filtering
Pour it through a paper filter or a fine cloth. It's slow, but it's the difference between a clean cup and sludge at the bottom of your glass.
8:30 Storing and serving
Cold brew keeps for about a week in a sealed jar. Serve it over ice, add milk if you like, and taste before you add any sugar.
